What FridgeCook does

Photograph your fridge, a pantry shelf, or the bag you just brought home. FridgeCook reads the photo on this iPhone and lists what it sees: the name of each item, roughly how much of it there is, and a freshness hint.

The hint is the part that helps. It is one of four things:

  • Use soon — the app thinks this should be cooked first.
  • Looks fine — nothing to hurry.
  • Opened — it can see the pack is open.
  • Best by … — a date it read on the packaging.

Then it shows you what you could cook tonight, with the food that needs using first at the top. Every recipe is original and was written for this app. The shelf-life figures behind the ranking come from USDA FoodKeeper, which is in the public domain.

How to use it

  1. Open FridgeCook and tap Snap your fridge.
  2. Take a photo of your fridge, a shelf, or today’s shopping.
  3. Check what it found. Tap × on anything that is wrong, tap a name to rename it, and add whatever it missed with Add something hidden.
  4. Tap Show recipes.
  5. Cook one, then tap Cooked it so the app knows what to use up next.
  6. Use the List tab for the ingredients you are missing. Anything a recipe needs can be added to the list with one tap.

Your photos

Your photo is read on this iPhone and then discarded. It is never saved in the app, never stored anywhere, and never sent to us or to anyone else. There is no account to make and nothing to sign in to.

FridgeCook collects nothing about you: no analytics, no tracking, no crash reports, and no third-party tools that gather data. Read the privacy page for the full account.

Getting started

The first time you open FridgeCook, it downloads the photo-recognition pack — the piece that does the reading. It is about 471 MB, it comes from Apple over Wi-Fi, and the screen shows you the size, a progress bar, and how far along it is.

You do not have to wait for it. You can browse sample recipes while it downloads, and if it stops you can resume it, retry it, or delete it from Settings.

Once it is ready, FridgeCook works with no connection at all — it never needs one again.

On an iPhone that cannot run photo recognition, the Snap tab is an ingredient picker instead: tap what you have under What do you have?, and you get exactly the same recipes.

Questions

Does it need an account?

No. There is no sign-in, no account and no subscription. You buy the app once from the App Store and that is the whole of it.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once the photo-recognition pack has finished downloading, FridgeCook reads photos, ranks recipes and keeps your list entirely on your iPhone. You can cook from it on a plane.

What happens to my photos?

They are read on your iPhone and then thrown away. They are never written to the app’s storage, never uploaded, and never shared with anyone.

Why did it miss something?

A fridge is a hard photograph to read. Things hide behind other things, lids hide what is inside, and similar packaging looks the same in a photo. Fixing it takes one tap: tap × to remove an item, tap the name to rename it, or add what it missed by hand.

How do I change the dinner reminder?

Open Settings, then Reminders. The reminder is off until you turn it on, and the app only asks for notification permission at the moment you switch it on.

How do I delete the photo-recognition pack to free space?

Open Settings, then Photo recognition, then tap Delete pack. The app goes back to the ingredient picker, and you can re-download the pack whenever you want.

Why is there no expiry date on each item?

Because a date on every item would be a guess. FridgeCook gives you a freshness hint instead, and uses public shelf-life figures to decide what needs using first. Those figures come from USDA FoodKeeper, which is in the public domain.

Can I use it without photo recognition?

Yes. On an iPhone that cannot run photo recognition the Snap tab is the What do you have? ingredient picker, and the recipes are the same ones. You can also delete the pack in Settings if you would rather keep that space free.
