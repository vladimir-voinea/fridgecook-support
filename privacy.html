Privacy

FridgeCook does not collect data. There is no account, and nothing you photograph leaves your iPhone.

What FridgeCook collects

Nothing. That is the whole answer, and it is a deliberate one: the app has nothing to send data to.

  • No analytics and no tracking.
  • No advertising, and no ads of any kind.
  • No crash reports.
  • No third-party tools or libraries that gather data about you.
  • No account, no sign-in, no email address.
  • No server-side part of the app, so there is nowhere your data could be sent.

The app is paid once on the App Store and has no subscription, no in-app purchases and no features to unlock. Apple handles the payment; FridgeCook is not involved in it and sees nothing of it.

Your photos

A photo you take in FridgeCook is processed on your iPhone. It is never written to the app’s storage, never uploaded, and never shared with anyone — including us. When the app has finished with it, it is gone.

The one thing it may keep is a short list of your last snap: the item names, rough amounts and freshness hints, with no picture attached. It is kept only if you leave Remember my last snap switched on in Settings. You can switch it off, or dismiss the memory with Forget last snap, at any time. It is cleared automatically after a week.

There is never more than one of these lists: a new snap replaces the old one.

What leaves your device

Exactly one thing, and it happens once: the download of the photo-recognition pack, the piece that reads your photos. Apple delivers it through the App Store’s own asset system. It is a download of the pack itself — not of anything of yours.

Nothing about your fridge, your photos, your items or your list is ever sent anywhere. Every recipe you open, every hint you see and every note you write stays on the device. After that download, the app works with no connection at all.

Notifications

The pre-dinner reminder is off by default. When you turn it on, the app schedules a local notification on your iPhone and asks for notification permission at that moment, and not before. If you turn it off again, the reminder stops.

The notification itself carries nothing about you or your kitchen. It simply says it is time to think about dinner.

Widgets and shortcuts

The Snap fridge widget reads two things from the shared storage area that the app and the widget both use on your iPhone: how many items are in your fridge, and how long ago your last snap was. It reads that area and never writes to it, and it sends nothing anywhere.

The What can I cook? shortcut and the Snap my fridge shortcut simply open the app. They ask for no permission and carry no data.

Children

FridgeCook collects no data from anyone, including children. There is no sign-in, so there is no account to be of an age, and nothing in the app is directed at children.

Changes
